## Construction rules to reuse

1. Start with the user's original complete reference and an actually assembled male/female runtime pair, not isolated attractive heads. Record skull, eye, ear, jaw, anatomical neck socket, shoulder, hand, and foot landmarks. Use the same assembled reference when authoring equipment or outfit changes. Compare whole figures at ONE body/display scale; do not align head and body crops independently and report the combination as correct.
2. Keep head geometry fixed within each preset. Hairstyles change hair, not face shape, eye position, skull size, or perspective. Long hair requires explicit front/rear ownership instead of a larger independently centered crop.
3. Preserve full source canvases and transparent margins. Cropping for an atlas must retain original size, trim offset, pivot, direction, and attachment metadata. Never automatically center each piece by its alpha bounding box; ponytails and asymmetric weapons make those centers misleading.
4. Keep one recorded anatomical neck socket and head placement across outfits for a preset and pose. Apply scale/translation to the complete head, hair, and facial-hair group; full-head helmets use the corresponding replacement group and the same attachment. The jaw and rear neck must meet the actual shoulder/garment seam, with no backward-projecting neck. Adapt a new garment seam to that template rather than introducing outfit-specific head scale or position. A new pose or direction needs its own recorded registration.
5. Preserve the native material ownership. Skin, hair/brows/beards, and irises use separate shade masks or explicit semantic layers; fixed ink and metal retain literal colors. One pixel belongs to at most one customizable material. Avoid guessing material ownership from the final RGB color of generated art.
6. Match the original complete reference's pixel scale, outline thickness, shading steps, perspective, and lighting. Preserve its smaller head/longer body proportions. Correct size and position together through the shared head-group transform, then inspect the complete figure. A smaller detached head still fails if the back of the neck extends behind the shoulder, the collar has a gap, or different pieces use incompatible shading and perspective.
7. Edit only the needed region when correcting a known part. Preserve source pixels outside that region. Retain raw output, exact prompt/reference identifiers, source hashes, registration metadata, and derived export receipts separately.
8. Use one action/frame/transform for adjoining layers during animation. Rotate a weapon about its actual grip; keep hands attached and verify blade orientation on both mirrored fighters. Two-handed silhouettes and flail chain pivots need explicit attachment treatment.
9. For the current arena rig, use one canonical face per sex. Derive hair-only layers from original material ownership, preserve fixed facial landmarks, and prevent source hairstyle faces from entering the assembly. Keep a protected face mask and an explicit final eye layer. Check the actual final-size iris pixels for every hairstyle and expression; an image changing somewhere is not evidence that the eyes remain visible. Remove unrelated source components and require a connected final head silhouette without floating pixel debris. Do not globally interpolate pixel art to compensate for thin feature loss.

The older fixed-head authoring system explains this discipline in [FixedHeadRig/README.md](<C:/Users/santa/Game Development/D-PIXEL BUILD/DPixelDemo_Modular_v002/SourceArt/ModularAvatar_v004_FixedHeadRig/README.md>) and [its authoring guide](<C:/Users/santa/Game Development/D-PIXEL BUILD/DPixelDemo_Modular_v002/SourceArt/ModularAvatar_v004_FixedHeadRig/Authoring_v001/README.md>). These are construction references; their older palette counts and IDs are not replacements for this browser game's current appearance contract.
